How I work

Built on a diagnostic mindset.

Every project starts the same way: with a question. What's actually failing? What does the system need? I don't build until I understand the problem — and I don't stop until the data confirms the fix.

01

Find what's actually being measured

My Thermogate held the wrong temperature until I realised the thermistor was reading the heat inside its own enclosure, not the room. The control loop was fine. The sensor was in the wrong place.

02

Assume the model is lying about fit

The planetary carrier bound on assembly: my screw heads stood proud of the face. A countersunk chamfer fixed it. No screen check would have caught it, and no amount of FEA would have either.

03

Load it past spec before you trust it

I ran static FEA on the trap's disposal-door actuator at three times its target load — 14.7 N — then cycled the mechanism 80+ times looking for failure. Predicting a margin and demonstrating one are different claims.

04

Write it down while you still remember why

I spent a summer turning machine-floor knowledge into revision-controlled standard operating procedures. Undocumented fixes get re-discovered by the next person, expensively.
