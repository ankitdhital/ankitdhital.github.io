4:1 Planetary Gearbox

I designed a 4:1 planetary gearbox in SolidWorks and built a hand-cranked assembly with PETG gears and an acrylic housing. The build included an exploded-view drawing, a bill of materials, and a correction for fastener clearance.

What I built

The gearbox uses a 12-tooth sun, three 12-tooth planets, and a 36-tooth ring. With the ring fixed, the sun-to-carrier tooth-count relation gives a 4:1 reduction. I designed the parts and assembly in SolidWorks, then produced the physical build and its drawings.

Design constraints

The gear module, center distances, and carrier positions had to agree across both meshes. I also needed enough clearance for the shafts and fasteners so the printed assembly could turn without binding.

Fabrication and fit checks

I modeled and mated the components in SolidWorks, then printed the gears in PETG and laser-cut the acrylic housing. The design went through five to six iterations. Hand-cranking the assembly exposed screw heads that interfered with the carrier; I revised the holes with countersunk chamfers.

Results and limitations

The finished assembly passed my hand-cranked fit and mesh check. The ideal torque calculation is 0.61 N·m input × 4 = 2.44 N·m output, before mechanical losses. I did not measure delivered torque. The most useful lesson was to include fastener-head clearance in the CAD review rather than waiting for assembly to expose it.
