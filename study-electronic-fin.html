Electronic Fin Design

A transient thermal model comparing fin geometries in Python, used to pick a fin that stays safe under a heat load while minimizing mass and cost.

Topic Overview

A transient thermal analysis of an electronic fin, using a lumped-capacitance model — valid because the Biot number is under 0.1, meaning conduction inside the fin happens much faster than convection can carry heat away to the surrounding air.

Objective

Model the fin's transient heating and cooling response, then compare fin geometries and coverage levels to find one that stays under a safe peak temperature while minimizing material mass and manufacturing cost.

Method

The transient response follows an energy balance, Qin − Qout = mc(dT/dt), solved analytically for both the heating case (with a heat input Q) and the passive cooling case (Q = 0). I implemented this model in Python to simulate rectangular and triangular fin geometries at different surface coverage levels, comparing their temperature, mass, and thermal efficiency.

Key Insight

Triangular fins outperformed the alternatives, but high coverage could still push the transient peak above 50 °C. A triangular fin at 20% coverage held the peak to 46.3 °C, and was selected over the higher-coverage options for that safe peak temperature, its lower material mass, lower manufacturing cost, and a more stable transient response.
